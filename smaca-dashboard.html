Σχετικά με το SMACA

Τι είναι το SMACA;

Το SMACA (Smart Campus) είναι μια ολοκληρωμένη πλατφόρμα IoT για την παρακολούθηση της ποιότητας αέρα, της απασχόλησης, της κατανάλωσης ενέργειας και των περιβαλλοντικών συνθηκών σε έξυπνα κτίρια και πανεπιστημιακές εγκαταστάσεις.

Χαρακτηριστικά

  • Παρακολούθηση ποιότητας αέρα σε πραγματικό χρόνο
  • Ανάλυση απασχόλησης και ροής ανθρώπων
  • Μετρήσεις κατανάλωσης ενέργειας
  • Περιβαλλοντικοί δείκτες (UV, θερμοκρασία)
  • AI-powered προβλέψεις και συστάσεις
  • Συστηματική διαχείριση αισθητήρων

Χρήσιμες Πληροφορίες

Time Range Selector: Χρησιμοποίησε τα κουμπιά 24h / 7d / 30d στην επάνω γραμμή για να φιλτράρεις τα δεδομένα ανά χρονικό διάστημα.

Export Data: Κάνε κλικ στο κουμπί "Export" για να κατεβάσεις τα δεδομένα σε CSV format.

Alerts: Οι ειδοποιήσεις εμφανίζονται αυτόματα όταν εντοπίζονται προβλήματα (υψηλό CO₂, χαμηλή μπαταρία, αδύναμο σήμα).

Flow-based Movement (In/Out)

Decision: When is space actually being used?

What is this graph?
Y-axis (Vertical):
• Center line (0): Baseline - no movement
• Upward (green bars): People entering the space
• Downward (red bars): People leaving the space
• Bar height: Number of people (scaled to max value)
X-axis (Horizontal):
• Time periods: Each bar represents one time interval (e.g., hourly)
• Range: Typically 24 hours (00:00 to 23:00)
• Pattern: Shows when people enter vs. exit throughout the day
How to read:
Green bars (↑): People entering - shows arrival patterns
Red bars (↓): People leaving - shows departure patterns
Example: If a green bar reaches 5 units upward, it means 5 people entered during that time period. If a red bar reaches 3 units downward, 3 people left.

Occupancy Density Timeline

Decision: When is space actually occupied? (Discrete presence)

What is this graph?
Y-axis (Vertical):
• Values (0 to max): Total number of people present in the space
• Each number: Represents the occupancy count at that moment
• Blue area: Filled area below the line shows occupancy density
• Step pattern: Values change in discrete steps (not smooth curves)
X-axis (Horizontal):
• Time periods: Each step represents one time interval (e.g., hourly)
• Range: Typically 24 hours (00:00 to 23:00)
• Step chart: Values remain constant within each interval, then jump to next value
How to read:
• Higher blue area: More people present during that time period
• Flat sections: Occupancy stayed constant during that interval
• Vertical jumps: Sudden changes in occupancy (people entering or leaving)
Example: If the line is at Y=7, it means 7 people were present during that entire time period. The step pattern shows discrete occupancy levels rather than continuous changes.

Occupancy vs Energy Correlation

Decision: Are we wasting energy during low occupancy? (Red zones = idle waste)

What is this graph?
Y-axis Left (Blue):
• Values (0 to max): Number of people present (occupancy)
• Blue line: Occupancy trend over time
• Scale: Adjusted to show occupancy range (e.g., 0-25 people)
• Ticks: Evenly divided values (e.g., 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25)
Y-axis Right (Orange):
• Values (0 to max): Energy consumption (kWh or similar unit)
• Orange line: Energy consumption trend over time
• Scale: Adjusted to show energy range (e.g., 0-200 kWh)
• Ticks: Evenly divided values (e.g., 0, 50, 100, 150, 200)
X-axis (Horizontal):
• Time periods: Each point represents one time interval (e.g., hourly)
• Range: Typically 24 hours (00:00 to 23:00)
• Correlation: Both lines share the same X-axis to compare trends
How to read:
Blue line: Occupancy (left Y-axis) - number of people
Orange line: Energy consumption (right Y-axis) - kWh
• Parallel movement: If both lines move together, energy use is proportional to occupancy (efficient)
• Divergence: If energy stays high while occupancy drops, energy is being wasted
• Red highlighted areas: Periods with low occupancy (<20%) but high energy (>30% of max) - potential waste
Example: If occupancy is at 5 people (blue line at Y=5) but energy is at 150 kWh (orange line at Y=150), this suggests energy waste during low occupancy periods.

UV Index Gauge

What is this gauge?
UV Index Scale:
• Range: 0 to 11+ (maximum UV index scale)
• Current value: Displayed in the center (e.g., 6.5)
• Gauge arc: Semi-circular gauge showing current UV level
• Color coding: Green (low), Orange (moderate), Red (high)
UV Index Levels:
0-2: Low
3-5: Moderate
6-7: High
8-11+: Very High
The gauge arc fills based on the current UV index value. Higher values indicate stronger UV radiation requiring more protection.

Hourly UV Index

What is this graph?
Y-axis (Vertical):
• Values (0 to 11+): UV Index scale
• Each number: Represents UV intensity at that time
• Orange line: UV Index measurements over time
• Scale: Typically 0-11 (can extend higher)
X-axis (Horizontal):
• Time periods: Each point represents one hour
• Range: Typically 24 hours (00:00 to 23:00)
• Pattern: Shows UV intensity throughout the day
• Peak hours: Usually midday (10:00-16:00)
How to read:
• Low values (0-2): Minimal UV exposure - safe for extended outdoor activity
• Moderate values (3-5): Some protection needed - seek shade during midday
• High values (6-7): Protection required - avoid sun during peak hours
• Very high (8-11+): Extra protection needed - minimize sun exposure
Example: If the line peaks at Y=7 around 12:00, it means UV Index was 7 (high) at noon, requiring sun protection.
